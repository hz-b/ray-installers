    Undulator Source parameters¶

    What the parameters of the Undulator Source do, in full. The tooltip behind each parameter's information icon gives the short form, at most four lines; this page has the rest, and points to the pages that go into the physics.

    The first four parameters below belong to the accelerator and insertion-device block that the Gaussian Undulator shares, and read the same there.

    Accelerator Data¶

    by data file: the accelerator, its Twiss parameters and the insertion-device values are taken from the accelerator data file, selected with the dropdowns below this parameter.

    by parameters: there is no data file behind the source. The dropdowns are hidden and every value is entered by hand.

    Which file is used is set in Preferences > Linked Files > accelerator data, also reachable from the context menu of this parameter. Its name is kept as Accelerator Data File, read-only, and written to the beamline file, so that it stays on record which data a result was computed with.

    The file format and where its values come from are described in doc/AcceleratorDataFile.md in the source tree.

    Insertion Device Data¶

    by data file: one of the accelerator's insertion devices is picked from the selector, and its type, period length, number of periods and minimal energy follow it.

    by parameters: a device of your own. The selector is hidden and those values are entered by hand, while the electron beam can still come from the accelerator data.

    Type of Undulator¶

    horizontal, vertical, inclined: linear polarisation. circular, elliptical: a phase of 90° between the two field components. All of them are special cases of the elliptical undulator, with field components Kx and Ky.

    On "auto" the type follows the Polarisation Type of the selected insertion device, and the types the device cannot be run as are greyed out.

    Ellipticity Ratio Kx/Ky and Field Phase δ¶

    The ellipticity ratio r = Kx/Ky of the elliptical undulator, with Kx² + Ky² = K² kept: r = 1 is circular (at δ = 90°), r = 0 horizontal.

    The field phase δ between the two field components: δ = 0° is linear (inclined), δ = 90° an ellipse or a circle with its axes along x and y, and in between a tilted ellipse. The polarisation (S1, S2, S3) follows from δ and Kx/Ky. Together, Kx, Ky and δ cover APPLE-type devices of variable polarisation.

    Auto Photon Energy¶

    What Photon Energy becomes on "auto".

    spectral brightness maximum (harmonic): the harmonic energy, where the flux on the axis and the spectral brightness peak. Spectral brightness is flux per area per solid angle per ‰ bandwidth, the figure of merit sources are compared by; brightness without the adjective is the quantity integrated over the band (see doc/BrightnessNomenclature.md). Strictly the spectral brightness peaks exactly at the harmonic only without energy spread; with it the peak moves slightly below, far less than the flux maximum does.

    flux maximum through pinhole: below the harmonic energy the resonance moves off the axis onto a ring, and the flux through the pinhole peaks about one line width E/(nN) lower, with up to three quarters more of it. That energy is found by one urad run over the pinhole at 25 energies, some 0.1 to 0.3 s, with the electron beam and, when it is folded in, the energy spread.

    More: Field propagation in urad, §15.

    Electron Distribution¶

    The electron beam the source traces. The parameter belongs to the accelerator block; the Gaussian Undulator and the Dipole Source offer only its first two entries.

    manual: the beam sizes and divergences as entered. urad traces a beam at its waist with the emittance σ·σ′ and the beta function σ/σ′ of each plane; the entered sizes already hold whatever the dispersion adds, so no dispersion is added on top. (They used to reach only RAY's common block, while urad traced the lattice's beam whatever was entered.)

    derived by accelerator parameters: from the natural emittance, the coupling constant, the beta functions and the dispersion (Peatman, p. 27).

    zero emittance: no beam size and no beam divergence; the energy spread is kept. The sizes and divergences show zero.

    single electron: no energy spread either, the radiation of one electron. That shows the single-electron source on its own: on the UE65 at 300 eV it is some 35 µm wide in both planes, which is what Wigner (alpha) and the angular spectrum carry, and ray per electron incl. diffraction; plain ray per electron puts every ray of a single electron at the axis.

    Electron Energy Spread and Energy Spread Nodes¶

    urad is driven with a single electron, so the relative energy spread has no effect unless it is folded in.

    ignored: no energy spread.

    folded along photon energy: the field is computed at several photon energies around the traced one, σ = 2·E·spread, and their intensities are added with Gaussian weights. It multiplies the propagation by the number of energies, which the Fourier transform keeps affordable.

    folded, fast period sum: the same fold. It computes the geometry of every period once for all energies and steps the phase from one energy to the next, where the other recomputes both for every energy.

    Energy Spread Nodes: the number of photon energies the fold uses, odd, spread over ±3 σ of the photon energy spread. 3 nodes are the Gauss-Hermite rule instead: ±√3 σ with weights 1/6, 2/3, 1/6. The nodes matter only when the spread is folded in.

    More: Field propagation in urad, §10.

    Field Propagation¶

    Fourier transform: the back-propagation from the pinhole to the source plane as a convolution computed by FFT, some three orders of magnitude faster than the direct sum. It cuts the source grid out of its own output, so with the transform the source window is not an input, and it is hidden.

    direct sum: the original, exact to the sixth order of the path expansion, and by far the slowest step of a trace.

    More: Field propagation in urad, §§2–6.

    Ray Generation¶

    How a ray is generated from urad's field: its origin and its direction.

    angular spectrum (the default): the direction is drawn from |FFT(E)|² of the pinhole field, with the electron beam's angular spread folded in, and the origin from the back-propagated source. Source size, divergence and waist are right; the pinhole pattern is smeared by the source size. It costs two transforms per photon energy.

    Wigner (alpha): a coupled pair, the origin and the pinhole point drawn together, from a non-negative coupling whose two marginals are the intensities urad computes and whose correlation comes from the Wigner distribution. The ray then arrives at the pinhole point exactly, so the pattern and the source size both hold by construction and the waist is at z = 0, at the price of a divergence some 2 % narrow and some ten to thirty seconds per photon energy.

    ray per electron: urad radiates one electron at a time, each drawn from the beam with its energy deviation, each to one point drawn in the pinhole; the electron becomes a ray from where it crosses the undulator centre to that point. Divergence and energy spread are exact without any fold. The electron's ray carries the electron beam's size only: the single-electron source, which only the back-propagation knows, is missing. For comparison, and with BRILL's urad_get_photons, which does the same.

    ray per electron incl. diffraction: the same electrons, with the single-electron source on top (below).

    The first draw, a point in the pinhole plane minus an origin drawn independently, has been removed. Two independent draws subtracted are not independent coordinates: it left ⟨x₀x′⟩ = −σ₀²/L, so the ensemble's waist sat at L·σ₀²/(σ₀² + σ_P²) rather than at z = 0, some 440 mm off, and the source size entered the divergence a second time instead of cancelling.

    More: Ray generation and the Wigner distribution.

    Ray per electron incl. diffraction¶

    Each ray of an electron is shifted, origin and pinhole point together, by a displacement δ drawn per ray from the single-electron source: the back-propagated, unfolded intensity of one electron's field, as a 2D map of the source plane, the same for every electron. The source size is then the electron beam's and the single-electron source's together, as the angular spectrum and Wigner (alpha) have it.

    The shift moves the whole ray rather than the origin alone: moving only the origin would tie the direction to δ, and pull the waist off z = 0 the way the removed pinhole difference did.

    The shift alone would count the single-electron source twice at the pinhole. At a waist σ_P² = Σ² + L²Σ′², so the electron's pinhole point already holds it, and so does its direction u = pinhole point − electron, by σ_r²/L². The directions are therefore narrowed about their mean, per plane, by s = √(1 − var δ / var u), some 0.98 at 10 m on the UE65 at 300 eV. The pinhole pattern is then the electron's again in its rms, the divergence loses what it had too much, and the waist stays at z = 0, since δ and the electron are independent of u.

    What it does not carry: the narrowing holds the second moments, not the shape, and the correlation inside the single-electron source, the X its Wigner distribution shows, which only Wigner (alpha) has.

    Photon Weights and Electrons per Ray¶

    Both apply to the two ray per electron settings only. Photon Weights decides how much S0 each ray, each photon bundle, carries.

    Photon Weights = equal, by rejection: an electron is kept with the probability its weight gives, so that every ray carries the same S0. One electron in 15 to 30 is kept on the UE65. urad runs in batches until enough are kept, some 50 000 electrons a second.

    Photon Weights = unequal, S0 per ray: every electron is kept, and its ray carries the electron's weight as S0, scaled so that the rays together carry the flux. That is 15 to 25 times fewer electrons for the same number of rays, but the rays carry unequal S0 and are worth fewer rays of equal weight than their count, which the trace reports. The analyses weight by S0, so both settings give the same distributions.

    Electrons per Ray is the most electrons urad radiates per ray with equal weights. When the limit is reached first, rays are used more than once, which the trace reports. With unequal weights there is nothing to reject and it is greyed out.

    The electrons are also bounded by memory: every one is kept until the last batch, some 90 bytes each, within the budget Wigner (alpha) has as well, an eighth of the machine's memory -- some 20 million electrons on 16 GB, six minutes or so. RAYUI_URAD_COUPLING_MB sets the budget. At 400 eV on the UE65 one electron in 30 is kept, so 200 000 rays take some 6 million.

    More: Ray generation and the Wigner distribution, §8.

    Undulator Ends¶

    hard: exactly the number of periods, as urad always had it.

    1/4-3/4 end poles: end poles at 3/4 and 1/4 of the peak field, which zero the first field integral and centre the oscillation (Clarke, The Science and Technology of Undulators and Wigglers, 2004, sec. 7.5.1).

    soft (as SRW): the field continued past its last zero crossing and damped by a Gaussian, as SRW ends an undulator.

    Above the resonance the ends change the source by 10 to 20 %. They are tracked apart from the period sum and cost time.

    More: Field propagation in urad, §11.
