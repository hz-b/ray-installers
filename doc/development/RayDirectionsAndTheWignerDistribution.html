    Ray generation and the Wigner distribution¶

    How the Undulator Source turns a computed field into rays, why the obvious way cannot be right, and what the three settings of Ray Generation do about it.

    This supersedes §13 of FieldPropagationInUrad.md, which noted the idea while it was still open. Everything below is implemented and measured, on the UE65 of BESSY III and a soft X-ray beamline behind it.

    Tutorial: which setting, and what it costs¶

    The source parameter Ray Generation has three settings. A fourth, the pinhole difference -- a point drawn in the pinhole plane minus an origin drawn apart from it, what RAY-UI produced first -- has been removed: it put the waist some 440 mm off and counted the source size into the divergence twice (§1). The measurements below keep it for comparison.

    angular spectrum — the default. The direction is drawn from |FFT(E)|² of the pinhole field, with the electron beam's angular spread folded in, and the origin from the back-propagated source. Costs two transforms per photon energy, a few tenths of a second. It is what shadow4 does in its best setting (flag_size = 2: the size back-propagated, the directions from the far field) and in spirit what xrt does (directions from the far field, the size a Gaussian); it gets the source size, the divergence and the waist right and smears the pinhole pattern by the source size.

    A file that stores no Ray Generation -- one saved before the parameter existed, or with its forerunner Ray Directions -- is traced with the angular spectrum.

    Wigner (alpha) — a coupled pair: the origin and the pinhole point are drawn together from a non-negative coupling built from the Wigner distribution. Costs the distribution (0.4 s) plus the coupling (2.1 s) per photon energy. Take it when the pinhole pattern, the source size and the position of the waist all matter at once — which is the case whenever an aperture downstream cuts into the beam, because then the pattern decides what passes.

    ray per electron — urad radiates one electron at a time, each drawn from the beam with its position, angle and energy deviation, each to one point of its own in the pinhole; the electron becomes a ray from where it crosses the undulator centre to that point. No fold: the energy spread and the flux come out of the electrons themselves. The electron's ray carries only the electron beam's size. ray per electron incl. diffraction shifts each ray by a draw from the single-electron source and narrows the directions by the part of it they already hold, which gets the source size right and keeps the pattern (§8). With equal weights it costs some 20 s for 50 000 rays, about twenty electrons per ray kept; with Photon Weights = unequal, S0 per ray every electron is a ray and it costs a twentieth of that, with rays of unequal S0. Take it where the energy spread matters, as a check on the others.

    What each one gets right, measured:

    pinhole pattern source size divergence waist
    pinhole difference (removed) exact exact +1.3 % 440 mm off
    angular spectrum smeared by the source size exact -0.3 % 5 mm
    Wigner (alpha) exact exact -2.0 % 14 mm
    ray per electron incl. diffraction exact in its rms, energy spread included exact see §8 0
    ray per electron exact, energy spread included electron beam only see §8 0

    The divergences are against the angular spectrum of the same field, which agrees with an independent transform to 0.01 %. The waists are measured by RAY-UI on the traced rays (focus_horizontal_z0).

    Through a soft X-ray beamline at 400 eV Wigner (alpha) gives a 6 % narrower horizontal focus than the pinhole difference, with the flux unchanged to 0.8 %.

    Two things to know before using it:

    • Wigner (alpha) refuses to trace if urad could not produce the coupling, rather than falling back to another draw. A trace that produced nothing is a question; one that quietly used a different draw is a wrong answer. The refusal holds for that trace alone: its rays are all lost, the reason is printed, and Number of Rays is left as it is. (It used to zero Number of Rays from the next trace on, for good, whatever Ray Generation was then set to.) Ray per electron and an empty angular spectrum refuse the same way.
    • The coupling and the distribution cost memory and time: the coupling 16 bytes per source point and pinhole point and some 1.4 us per cell for its Sinkhorn solves, the distribution (source points)² × angles² terms, 1e10 in 16 s on four cores. On the UE65's 151 x 151 pinhole grid against its 45 x 31 source grid that was 737 MB and a minute. So the coupling is taken on every k-th pinhole point, the smallest k for which the memory fits an eighth of the machine's memory (RAYUI_URAD_COUPLING_MB), the coupling stays under 1.6e7 cells (RAYUI_URAD_COUPLING_MAXCELLS, some 20 s) and the distribution under 3e10 terms (RAYUI_URAD_WIGNER_MAXTERMS, some 50 s). The distribution's angles go with k -- their step was matched to the fine pinhole step -- so it gets k² cheaper as the coupling gets k² smaller. The fine pinhole point is drawn inside the coarse point's block from the fine intensity: the pattern stays exact at full resolution, only the correlation is resolved coarser. On the UE65 k = 2 with 51 angles, 187 MB, gives the source size, divergence and pinhole width of the full computation to 0.6 %, within the noise of 50 000 rays; k = 3 likewise. The source grid is cropped to the box that keeps 99.99 % of its intensity first; the trace prints what was done. It refused past a fixed 250 MB and a fixed 2000 source points before, which the automatically chosen grids passed as a rule.
    • The coupling is computed at the middle photon energy and used for all slices, so in a band run its marginals are that slice's rather than the weighted sum. Over 0.07 % that is negligible; over a wide band it is not.

    To look at any of it, set RAYUI_URAD_GRID_DUMP to a path prefix for the source, pinhole and angular grids, and RAYUI_URAD_WIGNER_DUMP for the distribution and the coupling's diagnostics. The grids are tab-separated text with a header line; the distribution is a binary file with a text file beside it that gives its shape, axes and photon energy.

    1. Why the obvious way cannot be right¶

    A ray needs a position and a direction. The source has a field at the pinhole and, by back-propagation, a field at the source plane. The obvious thing is to draw a position from one and a direction from the other.

    It cannot work, and the reason is not subtle. Draw the origin x_s from the source distribution and the pinhole point x_p from the pinhole distribution, independently, and form the direction as (x_p - x_s)/L. Then the third distribution — whichever of the three you did not draw from — is forced to be the convolution of the two you did. A coherent field does not satisfy that relation: position and angle are correlated, and in the far field almost perfectly, theta ≈ x/L.

    So at most two of the three can be right, and which two is a choice:

    • Draw origin and pinhole point: the pinhole pattern and the source size are right by construction, and the direction pays. It pays twice over. The difference of two independent draws leaves <x_s theta> = -sigma_s^2/L, so the ensemble's waist is not at z = 0 but at L sigma_s^2/(sigma_s^2 + sigma_p^2) — 440 mm for the UE65 at a 10 m pinhole, measured on the rays. And the source size enters the divergence a second time instead of cancelling.
    • Draw origin and direction: the source size and the divergence are right, and the pinhole pattern is smeared by the source size — 61 um at 100 eV against a 31 um grid step, which is what takes the outer rings out of it.
    • Draw the pair: nothing pays. That needs the joint distribution.

    The joint distribution of position and angle is the Wigner distribution. Nothing less will do, and that is not a matter of taste: any scheme built on two independent draws is subject to the constraint above.

    2. The Wigner distribution, ported from BRILL¶

    urad_wigner_num in src/uradphase/urad_wigner.f is BRILL's undulator_wigner_num (Michael Scheer, for/urad_phase_main.f of 2026-09-14), ported. BRILL also has urad_phase_genpho, which draws weighted photons straight from the distribution; that route is not taken here, for the reason in §5.

    The caller puts the field in slot 1 of esour and its conjugate in slot 2, so that any of the four bilinear combinations of the two transverse components can be asked for; S0 is the two like pairs summed. It is driven from aradprop_u on the back-propagated source plane — not the pinhole plane — because the cost goes as nx^2 ny^2 ntx nty and the transform propagator crops the source grid to some 17 x 17 where the pinhole grid is 119 x 117: 1.7e9 inner terms against 2e12.

    BRILL's own documentation calls these calculations experimental, so nothing was taken on trust. Two things came out of checking.

    The odd separations carried the wrong phase. For an odd separation n the field product is taken at the points of separation 2*(n/2) — integer division — while the phase factor still carries n*dx. Half of all terms were summed with the wrong phase, and the distribution came out 17 % too narrow at 700 eV. Skipping the odd separations makes the two consistent and costs three quarters of the time. It costs no reach: the integrand needs the field at x ± s/2, so with the field on a grid of step dx the separation step is 2*dx at every output point, half grid or not, and the angular range is ±lambda/(4*dx) either way.

    And the skip did not take effect at first. The OpenMP directive says DEFAULT(PRIVATE), so the new flag — in the argument list but not in FIRSTPRIVATE — arrived in every thread as an uninitialised private copy, zero under -finit-local-zero, which is the old loop. The run gave numbers identical to the bit and the same wall time while the file header said otherwise. The loop now counts the terms it sums and the check prints them against the full loop: the skip has to show as a factor four, and it reads 0.2505.

    What holds, and is the check that says the transform is right: integrated over the full angular range the distribution reproduces the source intensity exactly — 61.34 against 61.34 um — and inside the angular window it reaches it reproduces the angular spectrum of the same field to -0.0 %.

    RAYUI_URAD_WIGNER_UP interpolates the field onto a finer grid first, which adds no information but lets the 2*dx separations reach the field's own Nyquist. It doubles the recovered ring minima from 7 to 12 and costs the fourth power of the factor — 16 times for 2. It is wanted only for drawing straight from the distribution; see §4.

    3. Why not Husimi¶

    Smoothing the distribution with a Gaussian of minimum phase-space area makes it provably non-negative, after which everything existing works unchanged. It is a dead end, and the number says so: the minimum blur it puts on the pinhole pattern is

    sqrt(lambda L / 2 pi)  =  140 um at 100 eV,  53 um at 700

    against the 61 and 23 um of smearing the independent draw already has. Husimi is a factor 2.3 worse than what it would be fixing, at both energies, because both quantities scale as sqrt(lambda).

    A sheared kernel, oriented along dx = -L dtheta, leaves one chosen plane sharp and blurs the source instead — but then the source distribution is plane-specific, and the point of a source is that it is not.

    4. The coupling¶

    urad_wigner_couple is the route that is taken. W transported to (x_s, x_p) = (x, x + theta L) gives the coupling the field implies. It is still signed, but far less so than W — 8 % against 36 %, the oscillation largely cancelling once an angle becomes an arrival point — and its positive part is the reference for a Sinkhorn projection onto the two measured marginals, which it meets to 5e-16. One degree of freedom is left, a tilt exp(-mu x_s theta) per plane; solving for the mu that makes <x_s theta> vanish puts the waist at zero.

    Both marginals are the unfolded, single-electron intensities, as W is. The electron beam goes on top, as a displacement of the drawn pair in position and angle together: the same transverse offset moves both points and the angular offset moves the pinhole point by itself times the distance, so the difference carries the angle and the origin carries the position. That is BRILL's genpho treatment and the only one that does not count the beam twice. The sigmas come from emittanceX/Y and the beta functions, which is where urad's own fold takes them from.

    Fifty Sinkhorn iterations, which is measured and not guessed: the marginal error is 5e-3 after three, 8e-5 after ten and 5e-10 after thirty, with the moments settled from thirty on.

    The coupling wants iup = 1 and is refused otherwise — its rows have to be the source grid the caller indexes — and that is the more accurate setting for it anyway, where the source marginal is met exactly rather than on a resampled copy. Measured both ways, the divergence is the same to 0.1 % and the source size is exact at iup = 1, for a fifteenth of the time.

    5. Why not signed weights¶

    Drawing straight from W and carrying its sign as the ray's weight is the only exact route, and BRILL's genpho does exactly that. It was left out here, deliberately.

    The negative share is 36 %, so sum|W| / net is 2.14 and the same noise wants 4.6 times the rays. That is affordable. What is not is the analysis chain: the rejection sampler cannot represent a negative weight at all (while (WI - rn < 0.0) never accepts one), and d_maxIntensity, the FWHM and the enclosed-energy widths all assume a non-negative profile. Linear operations are fine — reflectivities, apertures as masks, flux sums — and everything nonlinear is not.

    The coupling trades exactness for keeping all of that working: its 2 % narrow divergence is the discarded negative part, and that is the whole of its approximation.

    6. What it does to the beamline¶

    Measured at 400 eV with the real lattice beam, ten million rays, which puts 7200 at the focus and so 0.8 % on a width:

    horizontal width exit slit intermediate focus focus
    pinhole difference (removed) 7796 nm 518.1 nm 49.97 nm
    angular spectrum 7785 nm 514.9 nm 49.30 nm
    Wigner (alpha) 7309 nm 481.3 nm 46.68 nm

    Six to seven per cent narrower horizontally, at 15, 14 and 8 sigma. Vertically 1.2 %, which is 1.5 sigma and says nothing. The flux agrees to 0.8 %.

    The mechanism is not the one that was predicted. The focus position is barely moved — a 440 mm waist error through the square of the 150-fold demagnification is some 14 um, a fraction of the depth of focus. The error is in the size of the intermediate image: an object plane 440 mm off the waist is defocused at the exit-slit image, the beam there is larger, and that relative error is carried to the focus undiminished. The lever is the magnification to the slit, not to the focus.

    And it splits in two. At the source all three draws agree in size to 1.5 % and in divergence to 1.1 %, so neither marginal explains 6.6 % downstream. Of it, 7796 to 7785 nm — effectively nothing — then 7785 to 7309 for the coupling; measured against the default at a second ray count the split comes out about half waist and half pattern. The spectrum and the coupling share a waist position, a width at the waist to 0.4 % and a divergence to 0.6 % and still differ, so what is left is the shape of the phase-space distribution, which the coupling reproduces and the spectrum smears.

    7. What is open¶

    The 2 % divergence deficit. The coupling is 2.0 % narrower than the angular spectrum of the same field, and that is the negative part thrown away in the Sinkhorn projection. The default draw is 1.3 % wide, so the truth lies between them and nearer the coupling — but not at it. Closing this is the next piece of work and has not been attempted.

    The focus_* scalars do not close as a Gaussian. A waist 440 mm away with 40 urad of divergence cannot grow a width from 36.6 to 71.9 um, which would want 141 urad. They are used above for ordering and magnitude, where they agree with an independent measurement, and not for a quantitative phase-space reconstruction — that would want the rays rather than scalars.

    The band case. One coupling at the middle photon energy stands for all slices. Fine over 0.07 %, unexamined over anything wider.

    8. Ray per electron, and whether the back-propagation is still needed¶

    BRILL's urad has a mode the Undulator Source had carried as dead code since its first version: modepin = 1, one observation point per electron. Every electron is drawn from the beam, its field computed at one point, and the result recorded per electron in fbunch_u — the point, the Stokes parameters, the six field components, the Poynting vector, and now where the electron crosses the undulator centre. That is the Monte-Carlo counterpart of everything above: no fold, because the electrons are the beam, and no propagation, because each electron has its ray.

    How it is drawn. The observation points are not left to urad's uniform draw: uniformly over the pinhole, 95 % of the electrons land where the beam is dark. They are drawn from the pinhole intensity of the grid run, electron beam and energy spread folded in — the density the electrons give on average — with a tenth kept uniform, and handed to urad (irandom = 4, uradSetObservationPoints). Each electron then weighs S0/p, and is kept with probability w/max(w), so that the rays have equal weights. The flux is the mean weight. urad runs in batches until enough electrons are kept.

    Even so only one electron in twenty is kept, and that is the electrons, not the sampling: a single electron's pattern is sharp where the average is smooth, most of all the ring an electron off in energy radiates onto, and its weight there reaches twenty to twenty-five times the mean. Before the grid had the energy spread folded in, the electrons two or three sigma off in energy carried all the largest weights.

    Unequal weights. With Photon Weights = unequal, S0 per ray no electron is rejected: urad computes as many electrons as rays are asked for, and each ray carries the electron's weight as S0, scaled so that the rays together carry the flux exactly. The analyses weight by S0 — the histograms, the envelope and focus, the footprint power, and since this change the mean photon energy per bin and the Full Monte Carlo efficiencies too — so both settings give the same distributions. What the weights cost is noise: rays of unequal S0 are worth (Σw)²/Σw² rays of equal weight, which the trace prints. On the UE65 at 300 eV, 20 000 rays:

    equal, by rejection unequal, S0 per ray
    electrons computed 490 864 in 10.6 s 20 000 in 0.44 s
    worth equal-weight rays 20 000 14 769 (74 %)
    rms width in the pinhole against the folded grid +0.14 / +0.23 % +1.2 / −0.4 %
    rms source size, um (beam 17.90 / 1.89) 17.82 / 1.91 18.02 / 1.89

    Some eighteen times the effective rays for the same time. The differences of the weighted run are within the noise of 15 000 effective rays.

    What it gets right. The UE65 at 300 eV, 50 000 rays, 1.6 mm pinhole, energy spread folded:

    angular spectrum Wigner (alpha) ray per electron reference
    flux, ph/s/0.1%BW 1.2708e15 1.2708e15 1.2702e15 grid 1.2708e15
    rms width in the pinhole, um 204.4 / 200.1 192.8 / 187.4 196.5 / 193.0 folded grid 196.5 / 192.8
    rms source size, um 40.6 / 35.0 41.0 / 35.7 17.9 / 1.90 electron beam 17.9 / 1.89
    rms divergence, urad 20.0 / 19.7 18.9 / 18.4 19.6 / 19.3
    waist, mm 2 / 4 37 / 6 -3 / 0.7

    The pattern is the folded grid's to 0.1 %, with the energy spread, which Wigner (alpha) does not have: its coupling is the middle slice's, unfolded, and that is its 2 to 3 % narrow pattern here. The divergences are not compared against each other: an undulator line has sinc² wings whose second moment the pinhole edge decides, and the angular spectrum is not cut by that edge.

    What it cannot get right is the source size, and that answers the question. A ray from the electron to the point it radiated to is the geometric limit of the electron's field, and that limit has no size of its own: the source comes out as the electron beam, 17.9 by 1.9 um, against 41 by 36 um from the back-propagated field. Vertically the photon emittance is then 1.9 um × 19 urad = 37 pm, a ninth of λ/4π = 329 pm — the rays violate the uncertainty relation they are meant to represent. It is not a matter of the direction either: the Poynting vector of the electron's own field, traced back from the point, lands 0.16 and 0.06 um from the electron, two orders of magnitude short of the single-electron source.

    So the back-propagation is not needed for the pattern, the energy spread or the flux — ray per electron gives all three without it — but it is the only place the single-electron source size comes from, and no per-electron ray picture recovers it. Wigner (alpha) has it because it draws the origin from the back-propagated intensity; ray per electron would need the same, drawing a displacement from the single-electron source on top of the electron's own position, which is Wigner (alpha) again with the electrons in place of the fold.

    Incl. diffraction. ray per electron incl. diffraction does the first half of that: each ray of an electron is shifted, origin and pinhole point together, by a displacement drawn from the back-propagated, unfolded single-electron intensity, independently of the direction, so it is not yet Wigner (alpha) with electrons. The shift alone widened the pinhole pattern by 1 to 2 %: at a waist σ_P² = Σ² + L²Σ′², so the electron's pinhole point already holds the single-electron source, and the shift counted it twice. The directions are narrowed about their mean by s = √(1 − var δ / var u) per plane, u the pinhole point minus the electron, some 0.98 at 10 m; that gives the pattern back in its rms and leaves the waist at z = 0.

    On the UE65 at 300 eV the source comes out 40.4 x 35.7 µm, against 40.4 x 34.7 µm from the angular spectrum and 40.8 x 35.1 µm from Wigner (alpha); a single electron gives 35.1 x 35.0 µm against the angular spectrum's 34.5 x 34.9 µm. The pinhole pattern is the folded grid's to 0.6 %. The correlation inside the single-electron source is not carried. Plain ray per electron keeps the electron beam's size alone, as above.

    Two defects it found on the way, both in Wigner (alpha):

    • On the first trace after a load the coupling was checked against m_sourceGrid before it was built, found the placeholder's single entry, and refused. It is checked after the source grid now.
    • With the energy spread folded in no coupling was ever computed: urad_wigner_dump cleared its flag for the slices before the middle one and so for the middle one too. The flag is per slice now.

    And three in urad's modepin = 1, which nothing had run: the slot counter lbunch was shared by the threads without a lock; the emittance globals are converted in place, so a second run saw 1e-9 of the beam's (every run now starts from initUradPhase()); and with neither emittance nor energy spread urad collapsed the run to one electron, and so to one observation point.

    Where the code is¶

    • src/uradphase/urad_wigner.f — the distribution, the band-limited interpolation, the coupling, Sinkhorn, the covariances.
    • src/uradphase/urad_modules.f — fda_u/fdc_u and their accessors, iangdir/icouple.
    • src/uradphase/urad_phase_prop_fft.f — urad_angular_spectrum.
    • src/uradphase/urad_phase_main.f — the call sites, before the back-propagation overwrites arad_u.
    • src/Ray-UI/LightSources/undulatorsource.cpp — the parameter, randomFromAngularSpectrum, randomFromCoupling, the CDF, the electron displacement, runElectronByElectron, randomFromElectrons.
    • src/uradphase/urad_phase_main.f — modepin = 1: the electron at the undulator centre in fbunch_u(5:9), the slot per electron, irandom = 4.
