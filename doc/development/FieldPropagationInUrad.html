    Field propagation in urad — the direct sum and the transform¶

    UndulatorSource gets the ray direction from the radiation field at a pinhole some distance downstream, and the ray origin from that field propagated back to the source plane. This note is about that second step: what it computes, how urad_phase_prop_classic computes it, why the same thing can be had from a Fourier transform, and what is and is not approximated on the way.

    Both propagators live side by side. Which one runs is the Field Propagation setting of the undulator source, saved with the file:

    setting what runs
    Fourier transform the transform, and the default
    direct sum the original

    Section 9 shows how to run the two against each other.

    1. What has to be computed¶

    The pinhole plane sits at a fixed distance dx in front of the source plane, and both are flat. Given the complex field E(y',z') sampled on the pinhole grid, the field on the source grid is the Kirchhoff integral, discretised:

    A(y,z) = sum_{y',z'} E(y',z') * exp(i*k*DRRED) / DR * da / lambda

    with r = sqrt(dx^2 + (y'-y)^2 + (z'-z)^2) the distance between the two points, DR = r, and DRRED = -(r - dx) the path with the constant dx taken out so that the phase stays small. da is the pinhole area element and k = 2*pi/lambda.

    2. The direct sum¶

    urad_phase_prop_classic evaluates that literally: for every source point, a loop over every pinhole point. It expands the distance rather than taking a square root each time,

    eps = ((y'-y)^2 + (z'-z)^2) / dx^2
ans = sqrt(1+eps) - 1, as a Taylor series to sixth order in eps
DR  = |dx*(ans+1)|,  DRRED = -|dx*ans|

    and refuses to run when eps > 0.01, which is the range in which that series is good. The cost is O(N_source * N_pinhole). On the QMAP beamline it is about 80 % of a trace, and it grows with the product of the two point counts: a 205 x 205 pinhole grid onto a 256 x 256 source grid takes 100 s.

    3. Why the sum is a discrete Fourier transform¶

    The two planes are flat and a fixed distance apart, so dx is the same for every pair, and eps depends only on the differences y'-y and z'-z. ans is a function of eps alone, so DR and DRRED are functions of the differences alone. The kernel is a convolution kernel — at every order of the Taylor series, not just the first. The double loop is a discrete convolution evaluated pair by pair.

    Keeping the leading term, ans -> eps/2, gives the Fresnel kernel

    exp(-i*k*((y'-y)^2 + (z'-z)^2) / (2*dx))

    and expanding the square separates the three coordinates:

    A(y,z) = C * exp(-i*k*(y^2+z^2)/2dx)
           * sum E(y',z') exp(-i*k*(y'^2+z'^2)/2dx) exp(+i*k*(y*y' + z*z')/dx)

    What is left inside the sum is a discrete Fourier transform: multiply the pinhole field by a quadratic phase, transform, multiply by another quadratic phase. This is the one-step or single-FFT Fresnel propagator, sometimes called propagation to the waist; see Goodman ch. 4 or Voelz ch. 5.

    The transform pairs a pinhole step dz with a source step

    dz_source = lambda * dx / (N * dz)

    so the source plane it produces covers exactly lambda*dx/dz, whatever N is.

    4. Is the FFT an approximation?¶

    Three separate questions hide behind that one, and they have different answers.

    The FFT itself: no. Cooley–Tukey evaluates the discrete Fourier transform exactly, to rounding error. It is a faster way of computing the same sum, not a different sum.

    The kernel: yes, and less accurately than the direct sum. The transform keeps only the leading term of the path expansion, where the direct sum keeps six, and it takes the amplitude 1/DR as 1/|dx| where the direct sum keeps the full 1/r. These are the only two approximations the transform adds. Measured against the direct sum on identical points:

    pinhole grid aperture worst |dA|/max|A| rms |dA|/rms|A|
    51 x 51 1.395 mm 2.2e-09 1.1e-08
    99 x 71 1.395 mm 2.2e-09 1.2e-08
    205 x 51 5 mm 3.9e-06 8.0e-06
    205 x 205 5 mm 3.9e-06 8.0e-06

    The residual grows with the aperture, as it must: both dropped terms scale with eps. At the apertures the source is actually used with it is at the level of 1e-8 to 1e-5, far below anything the ray tracing is sensitive to.

    This is also not a fundamental limit. Because the exact kernel is a convolution at all orders (section 3), a same-grid FFT convolution with the untruncated kernel would match the direct sum to machine precision. It is not used here because the two planes have different grid spacings, and the one-step form is what turns that difference into the transform's own output grid.

    The periodicity: no, and this is the important one. A discrete transform is periodic, so the reconstructed source plane repeats with period lambda*dx/dz. That is not something the FFT introduces — the direct sum has exactly the same periodicity, because it is the same discrete convolution. The difference is that the direct sum lets the caller ask for a source window wider than one period, and then quietly returns replicas of the source. That failure was measured before the transform existed: at 800 eV with a 5 mm pinhole on 151 points the period is 465 um, and a 2 mm window produced five copies of the source at 463 um spacing, with the ray origins spread over all of them.

    In the transform the window is not an input at all. It is cut out of one period, so the failure mode cannot be configured.

    5. Both settings on the same beamline¶

    Traced on the QMAP source at 800 eV, 200000 rays, with the pinhole at 10 m on the 141 x 121 points its derivation gives. The core is read from the half width, the total from the rms, because the pedestal of section 4 moves the second and not the first.

    setting sigma_x core sigma_y core trace
    Fourier transform 27.1 um 25.3 um 22.3 um 19.6 um 17.0 s
    direct sum 27.2 um 25.3 um 22.4 um 19.1 um 21.7 s

    sqrt(beta*epsilon + sigma_r^2) with Walker's model 4 predicts 25.7 um by 20.0 um.

    Without the analysis the transform's trace takes 13 s. Its propagation is below a second, where the direct sum's takes 4 s; the rest is urad computing the field on the pinhole points (about 5 s, 0.3 ms a point), generating the rays (3 s) and tracing the beamline (4.5 s).

    The transform was once the slower of the two here. The source grid it makes is one replica period wide -- 1.089 mm against the 360 um of the window -- and the rejection samplers draw over whatever window they are given, so what the propagation saved, the sampling gave back. Since the transform cuts its grid down to the source (urad_prop_crop), that is gone. A third setting, "transform, then direct sum", ran the transform only to size the direct sum's window; with the crop in place it gave the direct sum's rays in the direct sum's time, and it was removed.

    6. What the transform gives up¶

    The source grid stops being a free parameter: its extent and step follow from the pinhole grid and the wavelength. That is the point — it is what removes the replicas — but it means PinHoleWidthSource, PinHoleHeightSource and the two source point counts are no longer honoured, and UndulatorSource reads back the grid the propagation actually produced. The panel hides the four in that case.

    modesphere places the observation points on a sphere instead of a plane. Then dx is not the same for every pair, the kernel is no longer a function of the differences, and none of section 3 applies. That case keeps the direct sum, which is why the transform is a switch and not a replacement.

    7. How finely the pinhole has to be sampled¶

    Both propagators read the field off the pinhole grid, and that field is not slowly varying. It carries the propagation phase from the source,

    k*(r - L)  ~  k*y^2/(2L)

    which winds faster the further out the grid reaches. Across the 2.4 x 2.4 mm pinhole at 10 m at 292 eV that is about seventeen full turns from the centre to the edge, and twice that to the corner. Representing it needs more than two samples per turn:

    step  <  lambda*L / (2*y_max)  =  lambda*L / aperture

    On that geometry the limit is 17.7 um and the 151-point grid gives 16.0 um, so it holds with about ten percent to spare. Measured on the dumped field, the phase advance per cell climbs from 0.57 rad at y = 0.2 mm to 1.4-1.9 rad by y = 0.6-0.8 mm, against the pi that would break it.

    The trap is that the step is fixed by the aperture and the point count while the limit shrinks as the aperture grows. Widening the pinhole to look at larger angles, without raising the point count in proportion, walks straight into aliasing: at 4.62 mm the limit is 8.7 um and at 7.2 mm it is 5.6 um, while a 151-point grid still delivers 31 um and 48 um. Nothing downstream notices. The field aliases quietly and the back-propagated source grows structure that is not there — a 7.2 mm pinhole at 451 points put a ripple of about 8 um into the reconstructed source at 308 eV, which is lambda/(2*theta_max) for that aperture and was absent from every properly sampled run.

    UndulatorSource warns on stderr when the condition is violated on either axis and says how many points it would take.

    The derivation behind the "auto" boxes of the pinhole parameters takes the condition into account. It sizes the aperture from the divergence and then asks for the largest point count of three separate conditions: sampling the far-field profile itself, fitting the source window into one replica period, and this one. They do not agree. On BESSY III this is the tightest — the UE65 at 800 eV asks 35 points for the period and 141 for the phase — but on BESSY II, whose electron beam makes the window ten times wider, the period sets the count.

    That was not always so. The derivation originally knew only the replica period, and the grids it produced aliased at every energy tried, by factors 1.0 to 1.6 from 100 eV to 2 keV. The two conditions are easy to confuse: both are sampling limits and both involve lambda*distance/aperture, but one bounds how far the source window may reach before it sees a replica, and the other how coarsely the pinhole may be sampled before the phase aliases.

    How much one period has to hold was overstated for a while. urad back-propagates the field of a single electron and folds the electron beam in afterwards, on the window, so what repeats is the single-electron field and not the folded source. The derivation asked for three windows per period, and on the BESSY II U49 at 300 eV and 4 m that meant 891 far-field points where 181 gave the same ray origins to the last bit; the copies set in only below 0.6 to 0.7 of the window, at 100, 300 and 900 eV alike. It now asks for one window per period. The transform cuts its own source grid out of one period, so there the period has to hold only the source, ten sigma of it.

    Note also what refining costs. The direct sum is O(N_pinhole * N_source), so doubling the aperture and keeping the sampling means four times the pinhole points and four times the run time. The transform is the cheaper way to afford a large, well-sampled aperture.

    8. How other codes do it¶

    SRW carries all of these forms and picks one per drift (cpp/src/core/sroptdrf.h, LocalPropMode):

    mode method
    0 angular representation — FFT to angle, multiply by the free-space transfer function, FFT back; output grid equals input grid
    1 propagation to waist — the one-step Fresnel form used here
    2 propagation from waist — the same in reverse
    3 propagation without the quadratic phase term

    ChooseLocalPropMode decides between them from the sampling of the wavefront. Mode 3 is SRW's characteristic one: the quadratic phase is factored out and treated analytically, so only the slowly varying residual is carried on the grid and the grid can be far coarser than the phase would otherwise require (PropagateRadiationSimple_AnalytTreatQuadPhaseTerm). SRW also keeps a direct Fresnel quadrature, PropagateRadiationSimple_NumIntFresnel, for the cases the transforms cannot serve — the same role the direct sum keeps here.

    SPECTRA computes the source profile from the far field with separable 1D transforms: one FFT per row, then one per column (spectra_src/source_profile.cpp, SourceProfile::f_SpatialProfileSingle). It also folds the electron distribution by multiplying with the analytic Gaussian transform exp(-sigma^2 k^2 / 2) in Fourier space (SpatialConvolutionFFTBase::GetFFTedProfileAt) rather than convolving with a truncated kernel in real space. urad's own real-space fold is cut off at 3*sigma: it leaves the outermost 3*sigma of its grid empty and, because the truncated kernel is not renormalised, loses the Gaussian's mass beyond it -- erf(3/sqrt 2) = 0.9973 per axis, 0.54 % in two. Inside raycore RAY-UI now folds the way SPECTRA does (urad_fold_gauss_fft).

    The one-step Fresnel propagator is standard and predates all three codes; the references below are the usual ones for the derivation and for the sampling conditions it imposes.

    9. Running the comparison¶

    Field Propagation selects the propagator for a saved run. URAD_PROP overrides it, and adds the comparison, which is a developer switch and deliberately not reachable from the interface:

    URAD_PROP=direct  the direct sum
URAD_PROP=fft     the transform
URAD_PROP=check   run both on identical points and report the difference
unset             whatever Field Propagation says

    check runs the transform first, because its output grid follows from the pinhole grid, then points the direct sum at exactly those points. What is compared is the two algorithms rather than two griddings of the problem. It reports the peak amplitude of each and where it sits, the worst and rms differences, and the timing of both. It also reports the phase step between neighbouring samples for the pinhole field alone and for the field times the chirp, weighted by intensity — the second must stay below pi for the transform to be valid, and on the QMAP geometry it is 0.06 rad against 1.06 rad for the field alone, because the radiation carries the conjugate spherical phase that the chirp cancels.

    10. The electron energy spread¶

    urad does model the energy spread: in its ensemble mode every macro-electron radiates with its own gamma = (1+dpp)*gamma0. RAY-UI drives urad with a single electron, though -- nelec = 1 and noranone = 1 -- which makes urad set ibunch = 0 and iemit = 0 and leaves dpp at zero. The transverse beam still arrives, through the analytic fold over y and z, but that fold has no counterpart along the photon energy. Varying the relative energy spread from 0 to 0.02 used to leave a trace identical to every digit.

    The fold does not need an ensemble. An electron with relative deviation delta has its resonance at E1*(1+2*delta), so at a fixed observed energy E it radiates as the nominal electron does at E*(1-2*delta). Folding over delta is therefore a Gaussian along the photon energy with

    sigma_E = 2 * E * sigma_delta

    which is how SPECTRA does it (SpectraSolver::EnergySpreadSigma, applied in SourceProfile::AllocateProfileEconv). SRW's srwl_wfr_emit_prop_multi_e with _me_approx = 1 treats the spread numerically in the same spirit, varying gamma itself and drawing it at random. Varying the photon energy instead is the first-order equivalent, and lets urad reuse one trajectory for all energies.

    With Energy Spread set to folded along photon energy, UndulatorSource asks urad for Energy Spread Nodes photon energies in one call, 9 by default, spaced evenly over +-3 sigma_E with the traced energy in the middle. They have to be evenly spaced: the direct sum advances the phase from one energy to the next recursively. The pinhole and source grids urad returns are summed across the energies with Gaussian weights -- intensities, since electrons of different energy radiate incoherently -- and everything downstream, the flux included, works on the folded grids.

    Two details make the transform safe for this. The source grid it lays out depends on the wavelength; it is taken from the middle energy, so the slice the fold weights most is exact and the others are off by (E_k-E_mid)/E_mid, a few tenths of a percent. And the crop that trims the transform grid measures the source on the middle slice, not the first -- the lowest energy, whose source above the resonance has a different shape.

    Measured at 307 eV, above the resonance where the spread matters most, on the UE65 at 10 m. The contrast is the mean intensity within r < 15 um over that between 35 and 50 um:

                                      contrast    trace time
fold off, direct sum                0.39         7.1 s
fold off, transform                 0.36         1.3 s
fold on,  direct sum                0.76        24.4 s
fold on,  transform                 0.75         5.5 s
fold on,  transform, 13 nodes       0.75         7.6 s
SPECTRA                             0.80

    The radial profile of the folded transform agrees with SPECTRA's to 0.91-1.02 across the +-50 um window SPECTRA covers; unfolded it was more than a factor two off in the centre. With the fold off the grids are bit-identical to those of the previous build, for both propagators; with the fold on and the spread at zero, identical to the fold off. 9 and 13 nodes agree to 9.4e-4. Both propagators fold the same pinhole grid, bit-identically, and their source planes agree to 3.8 % of the peak.

    The direct sum costs 3.5 times as much with nine energies rather than nine times, because each further energy only multiplies a pair's phase by a precomputed increment; the transform costs 4.2 times as much and remains the cheaper by a factor four. A folded trace with the transform is faster than an unfolded one with the direct sum.

    A third setting, "folded, fast period sum", gives the same fold for less. In urad_amprep the path, the arrival time and the distance weight of every period depend on the electron and the observation point but not on the photon energy, and the one-period amplitude does not depend on the period. The fast sum walks the periods once per point, keeps the arrival times and weights, and gives every energy a scalar sum of phase factors, stepped from one energy to the next on the evenly spaced grid; the per-energy sum recomputes the geometry and all six field components for every energy. The two agree to rounding, and a selftest holds them to 1e-9 of the peak.

    Measured, it saves little: on the QMAP source at 800 eV with nine energies, single-threaded, urad takes 9.1 s instead of 9.5 s with the transform and 21.1 s instead of 21.6 s with the direct sum. The period sum was not where the fold's cost lies. That is in the one-period integral, which advances all nine energies at every step of the electron's path, and in the propagation, which runs once per energy.

    What does save time is fewer nodes, and how few suffice follows from how the spread compares with the width of the line, 2 pi n N delta_rms: 0.44 for the UE65 at the first harmonic, 1.3 at the third. Against 41 nodes, as the largest deviation of the pinhole grid relative to its peak:

    nodes               first harmonic, 307 eV     third harmonic, 800 eV
fold off                   16.5 %                     32 %
3 over +-3 sigma           14.4 %                     31 %
3, Gauss-Hermite            0.20 %                     4.9 %
5                           0.12 %                     2.3 %
7                           0.16 %                     0.17 %
9                           0.14 %                     0.14 %
15                          0.08 %                     0.08 %

    Three nodes over +-3 sigma give the outer two a hundredth of the weight each and fold next to nothing in, so three nodes are the Gauss-Hermite rule instead: +-sqrt(3) sigma with weights 1/6, 2/3, 1/6, exact for polynomials to the fifth degree and still an evenly spaced grid. At the first harmonic that is as good as nine; at the third, where the fold lowers the peak by a third, it leaves 5 %, and seven nodes are needed for 0.2 %. The slow decline from seven nodes on is the end nodes at +-3 sigma taking full weight, a first-order error in the node spacing, and stays below 0.2 %. The flux through the pinhole changes by at most 0.04 % from three Gauss-Hermite nodes on, against 2 to 5 % for the fold off.

    The fold is off by default. Relative Energy Spread defaults to 0.001, so switching the fold on by default would change the result, and with the direct sum the run time, of every existing beamline.

    11. The ends of the undulator¶

    urad computes the trajectory and the radiation of one period, the one centred on x = 0, and adds it up over N periods: period i enters with the phase the electron has slipped by then and with the distance of that period from the observer (urad_amprep, the loop over nper_u). That is exact for N identical periods of B0 cos(k x) and nothing outside -- a field that switches on at full height and off again. It is also all urad could describe: the field routine bhalba_omp is B0 cosh(k y) cos(k x) everywhere, and the tracking range sets where it starts and stops.

    Real undulators end in weaker poles that bring the orbit in and out without a kink or an offset, and above the resonance the ends matter. The structure near the axis there scales with 1/N, and a few percent more or less effective periods move the source plane by 10 to 20 %. Measured with SRW and with an independent near-field integral on the UE65 at 10 m, 300 to 310 eV, with the energy spread folded in and the antisymmetric structure of the UE65's SPECTRA input: relative to hard ends, 1/4-3/4 poles change the flux in a +-40 um source window by 0.99-1.02 up to 301 eV and by 0.88-1.16 above, and the smooth termination SRW uses by 0.99-1.02 and 0.95-1.20 (srw/scripts/qmap_source_three_way.py and qmap_exact_near_field.py in the QMAP project).

    Undulator Ends chooses the end model:

    • hard -- as urad always had it. The default; the result is unchanged.
    • 1/4-3/4 end poles -- full height up to the last zero crossing inside N periods, then 3/4 of it for half a period and 1/4 for the next: the "1/4, -3/4, 1, -1, ..." end structure, the simplest that zeroes the first field integral and makes the electron oscillate about the axis (Clarke, section 7.5.1). SPECTRA builds the same for "End Correction Magnet".
    • soft (as SRW) -- full height up to the zero crossing a quarter period outside N periods, then the continuation (2 pi d/l)(1 - 4 pi^2 d^2/(9 l^2)) exp(-2 pi^2 d^2/(3 l^2)) of the distance d past it, with which SRW's SRWLMagFldU terminates an undulator (cpp/src/core/srmagfld.h, srTMagFieldPeriodic::compB). No publication describes it; it is cut 1.5 periods past the zero crossing, where it has fallen below 3e-5.

    Since urad's core is a cosine, both are the symmetric variants.

    The ends are not periodic, so they cannot go into the period sum. They are tracked apart from it instead, as an entrance and an exit section, each once per observation point (urad_end_setup, urad_end_profile, urad_end_field). With 1/4-3/4 poles the core shrinks to N-2 periods and the outermost period on each side joins its end section, which carries the 3/4 pole into it; soft ends keep all N periods in the core. The orbit on the core is urad's periodic one, z = z_amp cos(k x); integrating z'' = -z_amp k^2 p(x) back from the start of the core gives where and at which angle the electron has to enter, and the slippage it builds up on the way, which places the phase of the entrance section before the core. The exit section starts on the periodic orbit and takes the phase the period sum has reached. Each trace prints once how closely the tracked entrance meets the core and at which angle the electron leaves -- the check that the two parts join.

    The price is the tracking: a section is 1.75 periods long, so a point costs about 4.5 periods of trajectory instead of one. The ends are therefore off by default.

    Checked on the UE65 at 10 m with the transform:

    • With hard ends every grid -- pinhole, source plane and field, with and without the energy spread -- is bit-identical to the previous build.
    • The tracked entrance meets the core within 0.002 um and 0.1 urad, and the electron leaves the undulator at 0.02 urad (1/4-3/4) and -0.004 urad (soft), against an oscillation amplitude K/gamma of 410 urad.
    • Against the exact near-field integral of the same field (qmap_exact_near_field.py --field-structure symmetric), 300 to 309 eV, urad's single-electron field matches with either end model as closely as with hard ends: ring radius to 1 um, rms deviation 0.8-2.8 % of the peak, and the absolute flux density 2-3.5 % high, as it was before -- the tracking step of section 12, since corrected. The ring moves with the ends as it should -- at 307 eV the exact integral puts it at 361.4, 355.1 and 348.7 um for hard, 1/4-3/4 and soft ends, urad at 362.3, 356.2 and 349.7 um.
    • A trace on a 151 x 151 pinhole grid takes 1.72-1.83 s instead of 0.97 s without the energy spread, and 3.50 s instead of 2.18 s with it folded in over nine energies.
    • In the source plane, with the energy spread folded in and the same field on both sides (qmap_source_three_way.py --ends quarter|soft --field-structure symmetric, 290 to 310 eV, +-40 um window), urad and SRW agree with either end model as closely as with hard ends: urad/SRW 0.98-1.04 up to 301 eV and 0.99-1.11 above, energy by energy the values of the hard-ended comparison. The ends themselves move urad's window by at most 2.4 % up to 301 eV, and above it by 0.94-1.09 with 1/4-3/4 poles and 0.89-1.22 with soft ends.

    12. The tracking step¶

    urad tracks one period in steps of step mm (urad_e_b_field with uradstep_omp: an exact helix in the field at the straight-line midpoint) and integrates the radiation over each step with the exact phase, advanced by (1 - n.beta)*dt, but with the Liénard-Wiechert amplitude held at the end of the step. That amplitude is not slowly varying. It goes with 1/(1 - n.beta)^2, and 1 - n.beta swings between 1/(2 gamma^2) where the orbit runs parallel to the axis -- at every pole -- and (1+K^2)/(2 gamma^2) in between. The peaks are about lambda_u/(2 pi K) wide, 5 mm on the UE65.

    RAY-UI passed a fixed step of 1 mm, and the on-axis flux of the UE65 came out 2.36 % above the textbook, at 1000 m as at 10 m. A replica of urad's quadrature (QMAP project, srw/scripts/qmap_urad_quadrature.py) reproduces the trace to 3.5e-5 at 1 mm and converges on the textbook, to 4e-6 at 0.02 mm, as the step shrinks. The error falls with the square of the step and grows steeply with K (first harmonic, 65.6 mm period, at the resonance):

    K 1 mm 0.5 mm 0.2 mm 0.1 mm
    1 +0.28 % +0.035 % +0.005 % +0.001 %
    2 +2.35 % +0.30 % +0.042 % +0.010 %
    3 +7.66 % +0.69 % +0.088 % +0.021 %
    4 +19.1 % +1.25 % +0.14 % +0.033 %
    5 +42.4 % +2.13 % +0.19 % +0.045 %

    The third harmonic is less sensitive (K = 2: +1.4 % at 1 mm). urad's own physical constants, the CODATA values of 1998, play no part in the normalisation: they move it by 1e-7. One of them does matter at high harmonics; see the end of this section.

    At high harmonics a second limit takes over. The radiated phase turns E/E1 times per period, and the field of a harmonic is what is left after the contributions from across the period cancel: the weaker the harmonic, the more of the step error survives. At a fixed number of steps per period the error therefore grows with the harmonic, the faster the smaller K.     The step error depends only on K, the harmonic and the steps per period, not on the electron energy or the period length. With a 17 mm period at 2.5 GeV these harmonics lie between 6 and 49 keV; with the old 1 mm step they were off by up to four orders of magnitude.

    initUradPhase now sets the number of steps per period to max(2 pi max(K,1) 25, 30 E/E1): twenty-five steps across a peak and thirty per turn of the radiated phase. K and E1 are taken as urad takes them, from the fundamental harm/nharm or else from the field, and E is the highest photon energy traced. For K from 0.5 to 3 and harmonics up to the 25th the error stays within 0.12 % wherever F_h(K) >= 1e-3, down to harmonics some four hundred times weaker than the strongest; below that it grows (K = 1, h = 21, F_h = 4e-7: 2.7 %). On the UE65 at its first harmonic that is 0.208 mm, 315 steps per period instead of 67, and 630 at its 21st. Every observation point tracks them: a trace of the QMAP beamline (99 x 71 pinhole points, 1/4-3/4 ends, 200 000 rays) takes 12.2 s with the old step, 13.5 s with 315 steps per period and 14.7 s with 630, 4.5-5 ms per step per period. RAYUI_URAD_STEP (mm) overrides the rule. The selftest suite checks the on-axis flux against the textbook at K = 2 and K = 3 on the first harmonic and at K = 1.5 on the 21st (test/regression/tests/selftest/undulatorsource).

    That last case exposed the one constant that matters. urad derived K from the K-value energy with WTOE1, which was built on HPLANCK1 = 6.626176e-34, the CODATA value of 1973, while it radiates with HBAREV1 of 1998. The harmonic therefore sat 1.6e-5 below the energy it was set for -- 8939.855 eV for 8940 -- which is nothing at the first harmonic but 2.4 % of the line width at the 21st of 71 periods, and put the on-axis flux there 0.42 % high at any step (1.00422 converged, against 1.00113 at the resonance with the rule's 630 steps). HPLANCK1 in phyconparam.cmn is now 2 pi HBAREV1 ECHARGE1, the CODATA 1998 value. RAY-UI's C++ uses the exact SI values of 2019, which differ from urad's by 2e-7.

    13. Open: ray directions from the angular spectrum¶

    Not implemented -- a note of what the measurements point to.

    A ray's direction is taken from where it crosses the pinhole, x/L. That position also carries where the ray started, so the directions come out with the source size folded in once more, in quadrature: sqrt(sigma'^2 + (sigma/L)^2). The ray origins carry the source size already, so it is counted twice. On the QMAP source at 800 eV the horizontal direction core is 12.00 urad with the pinhole at 10 m and 11.73 urad at 25 m; sqrt(11.73^2 + (25.3 um / 10 m)^2) = 12.00, and the far-field value is about 11.69 urad. At 10 m the angular distribution is some 2 % too wide horizontally, less vertically, where the source is 19 um.

    Part of that is urad's own fold. The far-field Stokes parameters are folded with sqrt(beta*eps + L^2*eps/beta), the electron beam's size at the pinhole, which is right for the intensity there but carries the electron size into the angles; for directions only the divergence term belongs. The other part is the single electron's own source size.

    Moving the pinhole out is the expensive way round it: at 25 m the derivation asks for six times the points, and urad's field computation, about 0.3 ms a point, goes from 5 to 32 s.

    The cheap way is the angular spectrum. The single-electron field on the pinhole fixes the field everywhere downstream, and its plane-wave decomposition -- the Fourier transform of the field with the reference sphere taken out -- is the far field at infinity, whatever the pinhole distance. The transform of section 3 forms that product on its way to the source plane. Drawing directions from its square, with only the electron divergence folded in, would remove the double counting without moving the pinhole. The same step run forward would give the field at any plane downstream, the first mirror at 25 m for instance, from the 10 m field in milliseconds.

    To be settled before building it: how the fold of the electron divergence is split from the size fold in urad, and whether origins and directions stay sampled independently, as they are now, or are drawn together.

    14. Threads¶

    urad's field computation and the direct sum are OpenMP loops, but their !$OMP directives stay comments unless the files are compiled with -fopenmp. Ray-UI.pro compiles the four urad files, and only those, with it through rules of their own (urad_omp_module, urad_omp): -fopenmp implies -frecursive, which puts every local variable on the stack, and the legacy raycore sources are not written for that.

    The loop over electrons and pinhole points is collapsed (COLLAPSE(2), static chunks), so with the single electron UndulatorSource uses, the points are shared out. Each point is written by one thread only, and the grids are bit-identical for any number of threads. The per-thread copy of the field (fpriv) is allocatable, so it lives on the heap rather than on the 512 KB stack macOS gives a secondary thread.

    The number of threads comes from uradThreadCount in undulatorsource.cpp: - one when the trace's multithreading preference is off, or when a Looper job runs beside others; - otherwise OMP_NUM_THREADS where it is set; - otherwise the performance cores on macOS (sysctl hw.perflevel0.physicalcpu) and QThread::idealThreadCount() elsewhere.

    The first trace of a batch session reads the Background preference. On an Apple M-series Mac with eight performance cores, urad's share of a QMAP trace at 800 eV with nine energy-spread nodes drops from 6.3 s on one thread to 1.25 s on eight.

    On Linux and Windows¶

    State of 2026-09-13; built and run on macOS only so far.

    • Build. The rules call $(CC) -c $(CFLAGS) -fopenmp $(INCPATH), the same compiler and flags qmake uses for every other Fortran file. That is gfortran on macOS (QMAKE_CC) and gcc on Linux and MinGW, which hands .f files to gfortran. qmake's Unix and MinGW generators both define CC, CFLAGS and INCPATH. The OpenMP runtime was linked on all three platforms before: LIBS += -fopenmp for Linux and Windows, QMAKE_LFLAGS += -fopenmp on macOS.
    • Code. The only platform-specific part is the macOS core count, behind #ifdef __APPLE__. The preference switch is Qt, and the Fortran side is standard OpenMP 3.0 (COLLAPSE, private allocatables), which every gfortran from 4.4 on supports.
    • Stack. -frecursive puts urad's locals on the stack. macOS gives secondary threads the least, 512 KB, and runs; Linux (typically 8 MB) and Windows (1 to 2 MB) give more.
    • Verification. The Linux CI builds with Ubuntu's gfortran and runs every selftest suite on each pull request, so Linux is checked there. Windows has no CI and needs one MinGW build and a trace by hand.

    Open: on Linux and Windows the thread count includes the efficiency cores of CPUs that have them, Intel from the 12th generation on. urad gives every thread an equal, fixed share of the points, so an efficiency core holds the others up. Until the performance cores are detected there too (/sys/devices/cpu_core/cpus on Linux, GetLogicalProcessorInformationEx with EfficiencyClass on Windows), OMP_NUM_THREADS sets the count by hand.

    15. The flux-optimised photon energy¶

    On "auto", Photon Energy is the harmonic energy, where the flux on the axis peaks, and with it the brilliance. The flux through an aperture peaks lower. Below the harmonic energy the resonance leaves the axis for a ring, E_n(theta) = E_n/(1 + gamma^2 theta^2/(1 + K^2/2)). An aperture wider than the central cone collects more of it the lower the energy, until the line has moved down by about one line width E_n/(nN); further down, the amplitude off the axis falls. Auto Photon Energy chooses between the two: "harmonic energy" or "flux maximum through pinhole".

    The flux maximum is found for the source's own pinhole, derived at the harmonic energy or entered by hand (fluxMaximumEnergy): - One urad run. It covers 25 evenly spaced energies, from 2.5 line widths below the harmonic energy to 0.5 above, widened by three sigma of the energy spread. It uses 21 x 21 points over the pinhole and skips the back-propagation. Every further energy costs urad a few percent of the first. - Flux per energy. The flux of every energy is summed by the trapezoid rule. When the trace folds the energy spread in, the spread is applied as a Gaussian convolution of that curve, so the energy found is the maximum of the flux RAY-UI reports. - Maximum. A parabola through the largest value and its two neighbours places the maximum. A maximum at an end of the scan moves the scan.

    The result is kept until something urad is given changes, so repeated auto-calculations do not search again. The search runs before the trace sets up urad, which is why initUradPhase calls calcPhotonEnergy first.

    urad keeps its settings and its results in globals, so a trace and the search take turns on one lock (uradMutex). This matters in the window. While a trace runs on its own thread, the window re-runs the auto-calculation of the beamline on the main thread (propagateAutoCalculatedParameters). The first version of the search then rewrote urad's grid sizes under the running trace, whose electron-beam fold (urad_fold_gauss_fft) sized its arrays from them and asked for gigabytes. Now the trace holds the lock from initUradPhase to the end of its urad run. The search only tries for the lock, and while a trace holds it the last result stands. The search also writes nothing of the source: it takes the harmonic energy's pinhole from derivePinHole and sets urad up through setUpUrad, the part of initUradPhase that only fills urad's globals.

    Against a reference on the full grid (141 x 121 and 161 x 121 points) at 89 energies:

                                     harmonic   flux maximum   reference   flux gained
UE65 QMAP, 3rd harmonic, 10 m    800 eV
    energy spread folded in                  795.96 eV     796.02 eV     +76 %
    without                                  797.24 eV     797.27 eV     +84 %
U49 BESSY II, 3rd harmonic, 4 m  300 eV
    energy spread folded in                  298.83 eV     298.84 eV     +74 %
    without                                  299.15 eV     299.13 eV     +79 %

    The flux gained is that through the pinhole, against the harmonic energy. Coarser grids fail first on the flux: 31 and 41 points give the same maximum as 21, while 15 and fewer miss the flux by several percent. On one thread, one search takes about 0.26 s for the UE65 at 800 eV and about 0.09 s for the U49, and loading a file takes that much longer than with the harmonic energy. The search runs once per load. While a file loads, the source takes over the accelerator, the segment and the device in turn, each followed by an auto-calculation, and a search at each step would run on inputs that are not yet complete; two ran, and loading took twice as long. So the energy the file was saved with stands until the load is done, and the first auto-calculation after it searches.

    The selftest checks the property rather than a number. On the UE65 at its first harmonic (300 eV, 2.4 mm pinhole at 10 m) the search finds 296.45 eV. Traced by hand 1 eV below and above, the flux is 1.1 % and 2.0 % lower. With the energy spread folded in, the search finds 296.24 eV.
